## Comparison Summary

Both lists cover the same end-to-end journey (idea → stabilized STR → scaling). They overlap heavily on core workstreams: strategy, partnership, market selection, underwriting, acquisition, setup, operations, vendors, launch, and ongoing management.

The meaningful differences are **structural and granular**, not philosophical. List A is better organized for execution sequencing. List B is useful as a completeness checklist and for evaluating what a paid program claims to compress.

### Genuinely Important Gaps (List A additions over List B)

These are not rewording — they address real holes:

1. **Phase 0 entirely** — strategic decisions assumed settled in List B's execution-first framing
2. **Partnership governance** — death/disability, partner compensation for unequal work, future capital calls separate from initial contributions
3. **Entity structure for scaling** — holding company + property-level LLC
4. **Deal sourcing as a skill set** — cap rate, cash-on-cash, debt-service analysis; fixer vs. land-and-build vs. turnkey decisions
5. **Internet/connectivity underwriting** — verify actual availability, not just "listed as available" (critical for rural/national-park markets)
6. **Explicit downside scenarios** — recession, regulatory ban, occupancy decline, rate compression, maintenance disaster
7. **Specific trades** — plumber, electrician, HVAC, appliance repair (not generic "handyman")
8. **Launch as milestone-tracked phase** — first booking → first review → five reviews → Superhost
9. **Scaling standardization** — before property #2

### Unique Items from List B Worth Adding

1. **Voting rules** as explicit item (List A combined with decision-making authority)
2. **Underwriting model/template** as a buildable artifact
3. **Contact agents** as pipeline action (not just network-building)
4. **Turnkey** as explicit property-type evaluation (alongside fixer, land, unique)
5. **Local employer base** research (complements economic trends)
6. **Photography shot list** before photoshoot
7. **Build acquisition funnel** in scaling phase
8. **Properties #3 and #4** as explicit scaling milestones

### What Neither List Solves (Evaluate Separately)

A $13,000 coaching program's real value proposition is not information — it's **network, templates, and decision compression**. Use this consolidated list to ask:

- How many hours would you and your partner spend on items you can't shortcut?
- Does the program provide vetted vendors, attorneys, lenders, and underwriting templates?
- What's the cost of buying the wrong first property ($30K–$100K mistake)?

At 600 combined partner-hours, $13,000 ≈ **$21.67/hour**. The harder question is whether the program improves odds of buying the *right* first property.
